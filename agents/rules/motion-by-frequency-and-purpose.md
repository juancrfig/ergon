# Animations earn their place by frequency and purpose

Rule: Before adding an animation, name how often a user sees it and its purpose. Valid purposes include spatial consistency, a change of state, explaining a feature, press feedback, and preventing a jarring appear or disappear. Frequency decides: interactions seen hundreds of times a day (keyboard shortcuts, command menus, key navigation) get no animation, and keyboard-started actions do not animate; interactions seen tens of times a day are instant or barely there; dialogs, drawers, toasts and dropdowns use a standard animation; delight belongs only on rarely seen screens. Interface animations stay under 300ms (press 100–160ms, tooltips 125–200ms, dropdowns 150–250ms, dialogs, drawers and view changes 200–300ms), and exit is faster than enter. Use ease-out to enter or leave, ease-in-out to move, ease for color, linear for constant motion, never ease-in. Pressables scale to 0.97 on press; nothing enters from `scale(0)` (start at 0.95 with opacity 0); popovers grow from their trigger, dialogs stay centered. Name animated properties (never `transition: all`), animate only transform and opacity, and use transitions rather than keyframes for rapidly triggered effects. Stagger 30–80ms per item without blocking input. Reduced motion keeps opacity and color and removes movement. See the "Taste: motion" section of the `smooth-frontend` skill for easing curves.

Prevents: Interfaces feeling slow because frequently used controls animate unnecessarily.

Enforce with: For each changed animation or transition, identify element frequency and purpose; flag animation on keyboard actions or high-frequency elements, durations over 300ms on interface elements, `ease-in`, `transition: all`, `scale(0)` entries, center-origin popovers, animated width/height/padding/margin, keyframes on rapidly triggered elements, and movement that survives `prefers-reduced-motion`.
